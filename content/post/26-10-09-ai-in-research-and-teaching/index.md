## 🎓 Teaching

Ideas for teaching students to use AI responsibly: verifying outputs, reproducibility, authorship and disclosure.

- [Eight Rules for Teaching in AI World](https://kevinbryanecon.com/teachwithai.html) (Kevin A. Bryan, University of Toronto, August 2026): an essay arguing that AI breaks the link between assignment performance and learning, with eight rules for redesigning courses: decide which skills must be shown without AI, avoid take-home work that AI can complete, give students incentives to study, and state a clear AI-use policy (an example is included). The author founded All Day TA, an AI teaching company that the page uses as its main example.

## 🧰 Tools and skills

Useful tools, and reusable instructions ("skills") for Claude and other assistants that save time and improve quality.

**[Practical AI for Academics](https://claesbackman.com/aarhus/)** (Claes Bäckman, Aarhus, 27-28 August 2026): a two-day workshop with open materials, including slides, setup guides, written guides on agentic AI for empirical research and on verifying LLM output, and a `CLAUDE.md` starter template. The Claude Code skills (MIT licence) are on [GitHub](https://github.com/claesbackman/AI-research-feedback), among them a referee-style review of your own draft and a skill that builds a `voice.md` style file from your own writing. Written for economists, but the workflow carries over to epidemiology.

**Paul Goldsmith-Pinkham, series on AI coding tools for empirical research** (Claude Code). Written for economists, but the ideas carry over to epidemiology:

- [Getting Started with Claude Code: A Researcher's Setup Guide](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/getting-started-with-claude-code) (29 March 2026): installation, subscription options, data-privacy precautions, the context window and terminal setup.
- [From an Empty Folder to a Figure using Claude Code](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/from-an-empty-folder-to-a-figure) (29 March 2026): downloading Census data and building an R figure step by step.
- [From EDGAR Filings to a Structured Database using Claude Code](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/from-edgar-filings-to-a-structured) (29 March 2026): scraping SEC filings into a DuckDB database and running a descriptive analysis.
- [Writing and Thinking with AI Assistance](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/writing-and-thinking-with-ai-assistance) (12 April 2026): think before prompting, offload only routine writing, keep a style guide as a reusable skill and ask for margin notes instead of rewrites. It also points to the [strategic-revision skill](https://github.com/jusi-aalto/strategic-revision), which turns referee comments into a dependency-ordered revision plan.

**Repositories of skills and templates for Claude Code:**

- [MixtapeTools](https://github.com/scunning1975/MixtapeTools) (Scott Cunningham, Baylor University): tools for coding, teaching and presentations with AI assistance, framing Claude as a thinking partner rather than an autonomous agent. It includes audit workflows (a replication-style audit run in a fresh session, a review for overlooked problems and a bibliography checker), slide-making workflows and a `CLAUDE.md` template. It states "use freely", with no formal licence.
- [ChernyCode](https://github.com/meleantonio/ChernyCode) (meleantonio, MIT licence): a general software-development template that turns the Claude Code tips of Boris Cherny, its creator, into ready-to-copy configurations: project memory files, skills (such as commit, testing and code simplification) and subagents (such as code reviewer and verifier). It is not aimed at research.
